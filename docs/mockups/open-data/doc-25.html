Thammasat University Student Activity Regulations

Operation and Meeting of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan, B.E. 2569

Clause 1 These regulations shall be cited as the “Thammasat University Student Activity Regulations Re: Operation and Meeting of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan, B.E. 2569”.

Clause 2 These regulations shall come into force from the date the Council passes an affirmative resolution of approval onwards.

Clause 3 In these regulations

“Council” means the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“President of the Council” means the President of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“Vice President of the Council” means the Vice President of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“Secretary-General” means the Secretary-General of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“Deputy Secretary-General” means the Deputy Secretary-General of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

[หน้า 2 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 2 of the original]

“Member” means a member of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“Chairman” means the chairman presiding over the meeting of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“Commissioner” means an ordinary commissioner or an extraordinary commissioner of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“Office of the President of the Council” means the Office of the President of the Council of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“Office of the Secretariat” means the Office of the Secretariat of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“Sub-commissioner” means a person appointed by a committee to assist in any affairs of the committee.

“Meeting” means the meeting of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“The Assembly” means the assembly of the Student Council of Thammasat University, Tha Prachan.

“Council Area” means the jurisdictional area under the responsibility of the Council, venues utilized for meetings either permanently or temporarily, the offices of the Council Executive Committee, Committees, the Office of the President of the Council, the Office of the Secretariat, and members, and shall include user accounts, chat rooms, meeting rooms, and other platforms of the Council accessed via electronic media.

“Party” means a student party holding seats in the Council.

“University” means Thammasat University.

[หน้า 3 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 3 of the original]

“Rules” means the Thammasat University Rules on Thammasat University Student Activities B.E. 2563.

Clause 4 The President of the Council shall have charge and direction of the execution of these regulations and shall be empowered to issue subsequent regulations for the implementation thereof.

Chapter 1 Joint Council Coordination Committee

Clause 5 There shall be a committee designated as the “Joint Council Coordination Committee,” comprising the President of the Council, or a Vice President of the Council delegated by the President, to serve as the chairman of the committee, and not more than nine other committee members appointed by the President of the Council from party members. Such appointments shall consist of at least one person per party in accordance with the respective party's resolution. In the event that the Council is constituted by only a single party, the appointment of the committee may be omitted mutatis mutandis.

Clause 6 A Joint Council Coordination Committee member vacates office upon

(1) The expiration of the Council's term or the dissolution of the Council.

(2) Death.

(3) Resignation.

(4) Termination of membership.

[หน้า 4 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 4 of the original]

(5) The appointment of a new Joint Council Coordination Committee superseding the former.

Clause 7 The Joint Council Coordination Committee shall possess the following powers and duties

(1) To consider, proceed with, or coordinate matters pertaining to the meetings and affairs of the Council.

(2) To exercise other powers and duties as assigned by the President of the Council.

Chapter 2 Meeting

Part 1: Meeting Procedures

Clause 8 The meeting shall be held openly. General students may attend to observe the meeting within the Council Area as announced by the Chairman, unless a motion for a secret meeting is requested by members constituting more than one-half of the total number of existing members of the Council, whereupon a secret meeting shall be convened.

During a secret meeting, third parties are strictly prohibited from attending, save for those explicitly granted permission by the Chairman.

Clause 9 In the event of an absolute necessity to convene a meeting outside of the ordinary session, the President of the Council may summon an extraordinary session.

[หน้า 5 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 5 of the original]

Clause 10 The notice of meeting must be executed in writing, specifying the date, time, venue, and meeting agenda. Said notice must be communicated to the members in advance for not less than five days for an ordinary session, and not less than three days for an extraordinary session.

Should there be an unavoidable and urgent necessity, the President of the Council shall have the discretion to issue a meeting notice at least one hour in advance.

Clause 11 The Secretary-General shall distribute the meeting agenda and pertinent documents alongside the meeting notice. Should the President of the Council deem it appropriate, any additional matters may be incorporated into the meeting agenda, provided that such inclusion occurs not less than one day prior to the meeting date.

Clause 12 The issuance of the meeting notice pursuant to Clause 10 and the distribution of documents pursuant to Clause 11 may be executed via electronic media or alternative information technology platforms, as deemed appropriate by the President of the Council, with the exception of the delivery of classified documents, which shall be governed by the procedures prescribed by the President of the Council.

Clause 13 The meeting agenda shall be structured in the following sequence:

(1) Interpellations.

(2) Matters informed by the Chairman.

(3) Adoption of the minutes of the meeting.

(4) Matters previously concluded by the committee.

(5) Pending matters from the previous session.

(6) Matters proposed to the assembly for consideration.

[หน้า 6 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 6 of the original]

(7) Other matters.

(8) Scheduling of the subsequent Council meeting.

In the event that the President of the Council classifies any matter as an urgent matter, it may be placed in any sequence within the agenda; however, it shall not precede matters previously concluded by the committee, unless it entails the summoning of the President of the Student Union, the Vice President of the Student Union, an authorized representative thereof, or other individuals for the purpose of clarification or performance reporting, in which case the President of the Council may place it under (1).

The filing of an interpellation against the Council Executive Committee or a Council Executive member is permissible and shall be placed under (1), provided that the interpellation is submitted to the President of the Council at least one day prior to the meeting date. The party subject to the interpellation reserves the right to elect whether or not to respond.

In the case of a live verbal interpellation raised before the assembly, the subject matter must relate to the interests of the University, the students, the Council, or be an issue of urgent significance. Furthermore, it must not be framed as a personal attack against any individual or group.

The Chairman's ruling concerning the filing of interpellations pursuant to paragraphs three and four shall be deemed absolute and final.

Clause 14 Prior to commencing the meeting agenda, the Chairman may grant members permission to consult on matters concerning student grievances or any other issues, in accordance with the criteria and procedures prescribed by the President of the Council. The President of the Council shall subsequently forward said matters to the relevant University agency for clarification within the subsequent meeting and shall notify the members accordingly.

[หน้า 7 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 7 of the original]

Clause 15 Prior to every meeting, attending members are required to affix their signatures in designated registration documents or alternative formats, or authenticate their identity in accordance with the regulations prescribed by the President of the Council. A quorum shall be constituted when not less than one-half of the total number of existing members have registered their attendance.

Once the quorum is achieved and the signal to convene is given, the Chairman shall commence the meeting within five minutes thereafter.

Clause 16 Should a member fail to comply with Clause 15, paragraph one, without providing prior notification to the President of the Council or the Secretary-General, said member shall be deemed absent.

If a member enters or departs the assembly while the meeting is in progress, they must immediately notify the Chairman or the Secretary-General under all circumstances.

Upon the Chairman signaling the commencement of the meeting, all persons within the assembly are strictly required to maintain order and decorum.

Clause 17 Upon the expiration of thirty minutes from the scheduled meeting time, if the number of attending members remains insufficient to constitute a quorum, the Chairman may order the postponement of the meeting.

Clause 18 During said meeting, the assembly shall exclusively deliberate upon matters contained within the meeting agenda and must proceed in the prescribed sequence, unless the assembly resolves otherwise by an affirmative vote of not less than one-third of the quorum.

Clause 19 Any person desiring to address the assembly shall indicate their intent by raising their hand above their head. The individual may only speak upon receiving permission from the Chairman, and all remarks must be addressed solely to the Chairman.

[หน้า 8 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 8 of the original]

If it is necessary to invoke the name of another member, the speaker must expressly state "Request permission to reference a name" on every occasion.

For meetings conducted via electronic media, the Chairman shall prescribe appropriate criteria and procedures for members to manifest their intent to address the assembly.

Clause 20 The Chairman may consult the assembly regarding any issues, prescribe procedures for verifying the quorum, or order a recess, postponement, or adjournment of the meeting as deemed appropriate.

Should the Chairman depart the assembly without issuing any directive and without appointing an acting substitute, the meeting shall be adjourned. Such an event must be formally recorded, and the Chairman is obligated to provide a clarification to the members concerning their departure.

Clause 21 The Secretary-General shall act as the drafter and reviewer of the meeting minutes.

Following the Secretary-General's review and prior to submission for the Council's adoption, a copy of the minutes shall be made available at the Council Area or disseminated via electronic media or other information technology channels for a period of not less than three days, or by any other means deemed appropriate by the Chairman, to facilitate inspection by the members.

All meeting minutes must document the names of the members present, on leave, absent, the observers, and a formal record of the voting.

A member reserves the right to request amendments to the meeting minutes to reflect factual accuracy by submitting an amendment request to the Secretary-General. Should the Secretary-General fail to implement the requested amendments, the member is entitled to

[หน้า 9 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 9 of the original]

reaffirm the request by petitioning the Council for a ruling, which requires an affirmative resolution of not less than one-fifth of the quorum.

Clause 22 For any meeting minutes made available or disseminated for members' inspection, if subsequent amendments are executed—whether by the committee itself or upon a member's request—the committee or the Secretary-General must formally declare such amendments to the assembly during the session wherein the Council considers the adoption of said minutes.

Clause 23 Upon the Council's adoption of any meeting minutes, the President of the Council shall affix their signature as formal evidence.

Regarding meeting minutes that have been adopted but not yet signed by the President, or minutes that remain unadopted due to the expiration of the Council's term, the Secretary-General shall document the cause thereof and shall serve as the official certifier of the accuracy of said minutes.

Clause 24 The Council may resolve to withhold the recording of secret meeting minutes, either entirely or partially, provided that a general record of events is maintained.

Clause 25 The disclosure of secret meeting minutes shall be subject to the assembly's deliberation and resolution. Such disclosure requires an affirmative resolution of not less than one-half of the existing members of the Council.

Clause 26 The Secretary-General shall be responsible for publishing and disseminating the meeting minutes, excluding secret meeting minutes which the Council has resolved not to disclose.

[หน้า 10 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 10 of the original]

Part 2: Submission of Motions

Clause 27 A "motion" denotes any proposal intended for the Council to resolve or decide upon regarding the execution or continuation of any specific action.

Clause 28 All motions must be submitted in advance in writing to the President of the Council and must be seconded by not less than three members, except as explicitly provided otherwise within these regulations.

Clause 29 A motion proposing the establishment of a committee by the Council, or requesting any committee to execute activities, consider, investigate, or study any matters falling within the Council's authority and duties, if proposed by a member, must be seconded by not less than one-fourth of the total number of existing members of the Council.

Clause 30 A motion requesting a Council Executive member to vacate office must be seconded by not less than one-fourth of the total number of existing members of the Council.

Clause 31 A motion requesting a commissioner to vacate office must be proposed by the respective committee or affiliated office, accompanied by a written statement explicitly specifying the evident rationale to the President of the Council.

Clause 32 In matters implicating significant interests of the University or the students, or in instances of urgent necessity concerning student rights and welfare, a member may propose an urgent motion for the Council's immediate consideration. An urgent motion must not resemble the nature of an interpellation and must serve the objective of compelling the Council to undertake a specific action.

[หน้า 11 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 11 of the original]

Clause 33 The President of the Council shall possess the authority to determine whether any motion constitutes an urgent motion. Upon such determination, the President shall notify the proposer of the motion, inclusive of the rationale, within three days from the date of receipt of said motion.

The President of the Council shall place the urgent motion on the meeting agenda as an urgent matter.

For motions determined by the President of the Council as non-urgent, the President shall proceed in accordance with Clause 34.

Clause 34 Subject to Clause 33, the President of the Council shall place the motion on the meeting agenda within seven days from the date of its receipt, in chronological order of submission. The term "days" herein shall be construed as days within the meeting session.

Clause 35 The following motions are exempt from the requirement of prior written submission

(1) A motion to consider a matter as an urgent matter.

(2) A motion to amend the meeting agenda.

(3) Motions permitted by the Chairman as deemed appropriate.

Clause 36 While the assembly is actively deliberating a motion, the proposal of alternative motions is strictly prohibited, with the exception of the following:

(1) A motion to consolidate meeting agendas of identical, similar, or related nature for concurrent consideration.

(2) A motion to refer an issue to a committee for consideration, or to request any person to submit documents, provide factual testimony, or express opinions.

[หน้า 12 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 12 of the original]

(3) A motion to sever or consolidate issues for consideration or resolution.

(4) A motion to postpone consultation or consideration.

(5) A motion to close the debate.

(6) A motion to raise other matters for consultation or consideration.

Clause 37 The proposal of motions pursuant to Clause 36 (5) and (6) concurrently with one's own debate is prohibited.

Clause 38 In the event the assembly resolves to raise another matter for consultation or consideration, the original motion shall be deemed to have lapsed.

Clause 39 For motions submitted in advance in writing, the seconders shall signify their endorsement by raising their hand above their head.

In meetings conducted via electronic media, the seconders shall signify their endorsement in accordance with the criteria and procedures prescribed by the Chairman.

A group of not less than 25 students may propose a motion concerning a grievance or any issue for the Council's consideration. The procedures and formats for such proposals shall conform to the notifications prescribed by the President of the Council.

Clause 40 For any motion placed on the meeting agenda, should the proposer intend to withdraw the motion, make amendments, withdraw their name as a co-proposer, or should a seconder intend to withdraw their endorsement, the prior consent of the assembly must be obtained.

Clause 41 Once a motion has reached its respective agenda for consideration within the assembly, the proposal of an alternative motion sharing the same fundamental principle is prohibited. If the proposer or the amender fails to provide clarification before the assembly, or

[หน้า 13 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 13 of the original]

is absent without an authorized representative to clarify on their behalf, said motion or amendment shall be deemed to have lapsed.

The authorization of a representative to provide clarification must be executed in writing and submitted to the President of the Council.

Clause 42 Should a motion lapse, the resubmission of a motion sharing the same fundamental principle during the same meeting session is prohibited, unless it pertains to motions that have not yet been put to a vote, or motions expressly permitted by the President of the Council upon determination that the surrounding circumstances have materially changed.

Clause 43 Any motion placed on the meeting agenda that remains unconsidered by the Council during that meeting session shall be deemed to have lapsed.

Part 3: Debate

Clause 44 The party vested with the first right of debate is the proposer of the motion or the amender. Should there be multiple proposers or amenders, the Chairman shall grant permission to only one individual to initiate the debate.

A commissioner who has reserved their opinion, a commissioner authorized by a commissioner who has reserved their opinion, or a commissioner authorized by an amender who has reserved their amendment at the committee level, shall hold a status equivalent to an amender.

Clause 45 In the presence of multiple debaters, the Chairman maintains the discretion to permit any individual to debate, provided that due consideration is afforded to the proposer, the amender, and those who have not yet participated in the debate.

[หน้า 14 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 14 of the original]

Clause 46 The debate must strictly adhere to or be directly relevant to the issue currently under consultation. It must not be superfluous, circular, repetitive, or duplicative. The unnecessary reading of documents to the assembly is prohibited, as is the introduction of any objects into the assembly for display, unless explicitly permitted by the Chairman.

Debaters are strictly prohibited from exhibiting improper conduct or utilizing derogatory language, and shall not unnecessarily reference members or any individuals.

Clause 47 Should the Chairman determine that an individual has debated sufficiently, the Chairman may direct said person to cease debating.

Clause 48 Any member intending to protest an alleged violation of these rules or regulations must signify such intent by raising their hand above their head. The Chairman must afford the protesting member an opportunity to elucidate, whereupon the Chairman shall issue a ruling as to whether a violation has occurred.

In meetings conducted via electronic media, a member shall signify their intent to protest an alleged violation in accordance with the criteria and procedures prescribed by the Chairman.

The provisions set forth in paragraphs one and two shall apply mutatis mutandis to any individual who is the subject of a debate that improperly references personal matters or any other issues detrimental to said individual, including instances where they are referenced by name pursuant to Clause 19, paragraph two.

Clause 49 Upon a protest being raised pursuant to Clause 48, the debater may voluntarily retract their statements or comply with the Chairman's ruling. If the debater departs the Council assembly without retracting their statements in accordance with the Chairman's

[หน้า 15 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 15 of the original]

ruling, the Secretary-General shall document the non-compliance with the ruling in the meeting minutes.

Clause 50 The debate shall be deemed terminated when

(1) No individual desires to debate.

(2) The assembly resolves to close the debate.

(3) The assembly resolves to raise another matter for consultation.

Clause 51 In circumstances where the Chairman deems the debate to have been sufficiently exhausted, the Chairman may request the assembly to issue a ruling on whether to formally close the debate.

Clause 52 Upon the termination of the debate, further debate by any individual is prohibited, except in instances where the assembly is required to vote on the matter. In such cases, one of the individuals holding the initial right to debate on each motion shall be granted the right to deliver a final summary debate immediately prior to the assembly's vote.

Clause 53 If the Chairman issues a signal by striking a gavel, standing up, or employing any other appropriate method, the individual currently addressing the assembly must immediately cease speaking and be seated.

Part 4: Voting

Clause 54 In circumstances necessitating a resolution of the Council, the Chairman shall issue a signal to notify the members prior to the commencement of voting.

[หน้า 16 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 16 of the original]

Clause 55 Regarding the determination of a majority vote, should the assembly's opinions be divided into two or more factions, the faction securing the highest number of votes shall prevail.

In the event of a tie, the Chairman may either mandate a revote or prescribe an alternative voting methodology. Any utilization of an alternative voting methodology must be seconded by not less than two-thirds of the quorum.

The provisions of paragraph two shall apply primarily. Should a subsequent revote result in another tie, the Chairman shall cast the deciding vote.

Clause 56 Voting shall be conducted openly. However, if a member proposes a motion, seconded by not less than two-thirds of the quorum, requesting a secret ballot, the voting shall proceed secretly.

In the event a member proposes a motion for a secret ballot pursuant to paragraph one, should another member raise an objection, seconded by not less than one-third of the quorum, the right to an open vote shall be maintained as a privilege.

Voting by proxy is strictly prohibited.

Clause 57 Open voting shall be executed via the following procedures:

(1) Raising a hand above the head.

(2) Casting a vote with identified attribution via electronic media, as prescribed by the Chairman.

(3) Any alternative method deemed appropriate by the assembly on a case-by-case basis.

Regarding voting under (2), should the electronic media experience technical failure, the methodology shall be altered as prescribed by the Chairman.

[หน้า 17 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 17 of the original]

Voting must primarily utilize the method under (1). Methods under (2) or (3) may only be employed upon a member's proposed motion approved by the assembly, during a meeting conducted via electronic media, or in the event of a vote recount pursuant to Clause 59.

Regarding voting under paragraph two, if deemed appropriate by the Chairman, the Chairman shall appoint not less than three members to serve as vote counters.

Clause 58 Secret voting shall be executed via the following procedures:

(1) Inscribing a mark upon a slip of paper, which is subsequently folded entirely closed. Those voting in favor shall write "Approve", those voting against shall write "Disapprove", and those abstaining shall write "Abstain".

(2) Casting an anonymous vote via electronic media, as prescribed by the Chairman.

(3) Any alternative method deemed appropriate by the assembly on a case-by-case basis.

The provisions set forth in Clause 57, paragraphs two, three, and four shall apply mutatis mutandis.

Clause 59 Following a vote conducted pursuant to Clause 57 (1), if a member requests a recount, seconded by not less than ten members, a recount shall be executed by repeating the original procedure once.

If a member requests a second recount, seconded by not less than ten members, the voting methodology shall transition to the methods under Clause 57 (2) or (3). However, a recount may not be requested if the margin of victory exceeds five votes.

[หน้า 18 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 18 of the original]

Clause 60 For any motion lacking opposition, the Chairman shall inquire of the assembly whether any member holds a dissenting view. In the absence of dissent, the assembly shall be deemed to have passed a resolution approving said motion.

The Secretary-General shall compile a formal record of the individual voting of the Council members and shall disclose such record within the meeting minutes, via electronic media, or through other forms of information technology media, to ensure public awareness among students and the Thammasat community, with the exception of secret voting under Clause 58.

Chapter 3 Committees

Clause 61 Ordinary committees may be established upon the approval of more than one-half of the total number of existing members of the Council.

A member must be affiliated with at least one ordinary committee, wherein the Office of the President of the Council and the Office of the Secretariat hold a status equivalent to an ordinary committee.

Clause 62 The establishment of an extraordinary committee may be executed to undertake a specific matter falling outside the scope of authority and duties of the ordinary committees, or as the Council otherwise deems appropriate.

An extraordinary committee shall be dissolved upon the completion of its designated undertaking.

[หน้า 19 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 19 of the original]

Clause 63 The election of the chairman of a committee shall be conducted in accordance with the resolution of the Council assembly.

Clause 64 Committee meetings shall apply these regulations specifically concerning Council meetings mutatis mutandis.

The chairman of each committee shall appoint a vice chairman, a secretary, and other positions as necessitated, from among the commissioners within that committee.

In the event that commissioners are appointed to hold the position of vice chairman in a number exceeding one, or to other positions pursuant to paragraph two, the chairman of the committee must notify the President of the Council and the committee assembly regarding the necessity thereof.

Clause 65 In the absence of an individual holding the position of chairman of the committee, or in the event they are unable to perform their duties, the vice chairman of the committee shall assume the duties of the chairman.

Should there be multiple vice chairmen, the first vice chairman shall assume the duties of the chairman. If the first vice chairman is unable to perform said duties, the vice chairman in the successive order shall assume the duties of the chairman.

In the event that no individual is capable of performing the duties pursuant to paragraph two, the secretary shall assume the duties.

In the event that no individual is capable of performing the duties pursuant to paragraphs two and three, any commissioner may nominate another commissioner to assume the duties of the chairman, subject to an affirmative resolution by the committee assembly with a vote exceeding one-half of the existing number of commissioners.

[หน้า 20 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 20 of the original]

Clause 66 Upon a proposal to appoint sub-commissioners, a Council notification or a committee notification concerning the Appointment of Sub-commissioners shall be issued within fifteen days from the date of nomination.

Sub-commissioners are ineligible to hold positions pursuant to Clause 64, paragraph two.

Sub-commissioners maintain the right to attend Council meetings but are not entitled to vote on any matters of the Council. Furthermore, if the chairman of the committee or the Chairman deems it appropriate, sub-commissioners may be denied attendance at the meeting.

Clause 67 A committee possesses the authority to establish a sub-committee to consider issues falling within its jurisdiction and to report to the committee within the timeframe prescribed by the committee.

Should a sub-committee fail to complete its operations, considerations, investigations, or studies within the prescribed timeframe, the chairman of the sub-committee must petition the committee assembly for an extension of time.

The chairman of a sub-committee must concurrently serve as a commissioner.

Clause 68 Upon an ordinary committee's execution of operations, considerations, investigations, or studies regarding any matters within its authority or as mandated by the Council, said ordinary committee shall report the matter to the President of the Council for acknowledgment.

In executing operations, considerations, investigations, or studies, the activities must not duplicate those of other committees. Where such activities intersect, the President of the Council is obligated to coordinate joint operations among all relevant ordinary committees.

Clause 69 Upon the conclusion of a committee's operations, considerations, investigations, or studies regarding any matters, the committee shall report its findings to the

[หน้า 21 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 21 of the original]

Council by formally notifying the President of the Council, in order for the matter to be placed on the meeting agenda pursuant to Clause 13 (4).

Within the Council assembly, the committee holds the right to declare, clarify, or amend matters pertaining to the actions detailed in paragraph one. In this regard, the committee may authorize any individual to declare or clarify on its behalf, subject to the Chairman's permission.

Clause 70 Should the Council resolve to mandate any committee to execute operations, consider, investigate, or study any matter for completion within a prescribed timeframe, and the committee fails to fulfill the mandate within the allotted time, the chairman of the committee must expeditiously report the delay to the President of the Council, or petition the Council for a resolution extending the timeframe.

Clause 71 A commissioner vacates office upon:

  1. The expiration of the Council's term or the dissolution of the Council.

  2. Death.

  3. Resignation.

  4. The establishment of a new committee superseding the existing one.

  5. Absence from meetings for three consecutive occurrences without justifiable cause, and without providing written notification to the chairman of the committee.

  6. A resolution by the Council directing the vacation of office, pursuant to a motion under Clause 31, backed by an affirmative vote exceeding one-half of the number of existing members of the Council.

Clause 72 A sub-commissioner vacates office upon:

  1. The expiration of the Council's term.

[หน้า 22 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 22 of the original]

  1. Death.

  2. Resignation.

  3. The dissolution of the affiliated committee.

  4. A resolution by the affiliated committee directing the vacation of office.

Whenever a sub-commissioner vacates office by any means other than (1), a written report must be submitted to the President of the Council, and a Council notification concerning the Termination of Sub-commissioner Status must be issued within fifteen days from the receipt of said report.

Clause 73 In the event a commissioner position within any committee becomes vacant, the chairman of the committee shall hold the authority to appoint a member as a replacement commissioner. The chairman must formally notify the President of the Council of such appointment within seven days from the date thereof.

Clause 74 Attendees of a committee meeting, excluding commissioners and sub-commissioners, shall be classified as observers.

Observers maintain the right to attend committee meetings but possess no voting rights nor the capacity to execute duties concerning committee matters.

Observers are prohibited from expressing opinions within the meeting area or the committee's office, including channels via electronic media. However, this restriction is void if the chairman of the committee explicitly grants permission to the contrary, on a case-by-case basis.

Should the chairman of the committee deem it appropriate, observers may be barred from attending the meeting. The rationale for such exclusion may be provided subsequently, and the ruling of the chairman of the committee shall be considered absolute and final.

[หน้า 23 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 23 of the original]

Clause 75 A Committee of the Whole Council is defined as a committee comprising all members present in the Council assembly acting as commissioners, with the Chairman of the Council assembly presiding as the chairman of said committee.

Clause 76 A Committee of the Whole Council shall only be convened upon the Council's acceptance of the principle of any motion, provided there exists an urgent necessity for the benefit of the students, the University, and the Council, initiated by a member's request and seconded by not less than two-thirds of the quorum.

In this regard, the Secretary-General must formally record the urgent necessity described in paragraph one.

The convening of a Committee of the Whole Council shall be deemed synonymous with a Council meeting mutatis mutandis.

Chapter 4 Interpellations

Part 1 General Provisions

Clause 77 An interpellation is a question that a member has towards the President of the Student Union, Vice President of the Student Union, Council's Executive Board, members, or

[หน้า 24 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 24 of the original]

outsiders, depending on the case, regarding their duties, which may be asked in writing or verbally.

Clause 78 Interpellations are of 2 categories:

  1. Urgent verbal interpellations.

  2. General interpellations.

Clause 79 In raising an interpellation, the questioner shall be independent, not bound by any mandate or domination. Each interpellation may only be raised and questioned by one person.

Clause 80 An interpellation must not have any of the following characteristics:

  1. Being sarcastic, satirical, or malicious.

  2. Vague or difficult to understand.

  3. Being a matter that has already been answered or clarified that it will not be answered.

  4. Being a matter containing question issues identical to an interpellation proposed earlier.

  5. Seeking an expression of opinion.

  6. Being a question of law.

  7. Being a matter of no substantial importance.

  8. Being a personal matter of any person, except when related to work duties.

[หน้า 25 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 25 of the original]

Clause 81 Interpellations under Clause 80 (3) and (4) may be raised anew when the substantial importance differs, or the circumstances at the time of that interpellation have changed from when the previous interpellation was raised.

Clause 82 Interpellations raised by a member shall be reviewed by the President of the Council, and if there are defects, the President of the Council shall inform that member.

Clause 83 In each meeting, no more than three urgent verbal interpellations and no more than three general interpellations may be placed on the agenda.

However, if there are a large number of general interpellations waiting to be placed on the meeting agenda or general interpellations postponed from the previous meeting, the President of the Council may place more general interpellations than this.

The arrangement of interpellations in the assembly shall be ordered as follows: urgent verbal interpellations, and general interpellations.

Clause 85 The President of the Student Union, Vice President of the Student Union, Council's Executive Board, member, or outsider, depending on the case, who is being questioned must attend the Council meeting to answer the interpellation on that matter in person, unless there is an unavoidable necessity preventing them from answering, but they must notify the President of the Council of such necessity in writing before or on the day of the Council meeting, and specify when they will be able to answer.

In the event of assigning another relevant person to answer the interpellation, written notification must be given to the President of the Council prior to the interpellation agenda. The President of the Student Union, Vice President of the Student Union, Council's Executive Board, member, or outsider naturally has the right not to answer an interpellation when the questioner considers that the matter should not yet be disclosed because it involves the safety or significant interests of the University.

[หน้า 26 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 26 of the original]

Clause 86 Raising an interpellation must be clear, not excessive, circular, repetitive, or having the nature of a debate.

Clause 87 The questioner has the right to withdraw the interpellation at any time.

Clause 88 For any interpellation where the President of the Council considers that circumstances have changed, the President of the Council may order its removal from waiting to be placed on the meeting agenda, upon receiving the consent of the questioner.

Clause 89 An interpellation lapses when:

  1. Reaching the interpellation agenda of any person, if the questioner does not ask or is not in the assembly.

  2. The membership of the questioner terminates.

  3. The questioned person vacates office.

  4. The Council expires.

Part 2: Urgent Verbal Interpellations

Clause 90 For raising an urgent verbal interpellation, each member has the right to raise one interpellation per time on a meeting day, without prior notice, and it must have any of the following characteristics:

  1. An important matter of interest to students.

  2. A matter affecting the interests of the University and students.

[หน้า 27 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 27 of the original]

  1. An urgent matter. It is the power of the President of the Council to rule whether any interpellation is an urgent verbal interpellation.

Clause 91 In any meeting where there are more than three urgent verbal interpellations ruled by the President of the Council, the President of the Council shall draw lots to leave only three. Among these three, if it appears that there are interpellations of a similar nature, the interpellation drawn later shall lapse.

Clause 92 Urgent verbal interpellations ruled by the President of the Council under Clause 90 or processed under Clause 91 shall be placed on the urgent verbal interpellation agenda.

Clause 93 Asking and answering urgent verbal interpellations in a single meeting has a time limit not exceeding ninety minutes.

Any matter the President of the Council rules does not meet the characteristics of an urgent verbal interpellation under Clause 90, or is not permitted to be placed on the meeting agenda, or is not asked and answered for any other reason than a request for postponement by the questioned party, shall lapse.

If the member still wishes to raise it as an urgent verbal interpellation, it shall be proposed anew for consideration next time.

Clause 94 Each urgent verbal interpellation may be asked no more than three times per matter and must be completely asked and answered within a thirty-minute time limit, unless in any meeting there are fewer than three urgent verbal interpellations, in which case the time may be extended proportionally within a sixty-minute limit.

Clause 95 During the asking of an urgent verbal interpellation, if the President rules that any question falls under the prohibited characteristics under Clause 80, the President has the power to order the question to be changed.

[หน้า 28 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 28 of the original]

Part 3: General Interpellations

Clause 96 Raising a general interpellation must be submitted in advance in writing to the President of the Council, containing questions on facts or policies, and specifying that it be answered in the Council meeting.

Clause 97 The President of the Council shall rule whether any interpellation has prohibited characteristics under Clause 80 or characteristics under Clause 81, and upon ruling, notify the questioner within seven days.

Interpellations ruled by the President of the Council to be proper interpellations shall be sent to the questioned party.

Clause 98 Interpellations requiring an answer in the Council meeting shall be placed on the meeting agenda within fifteen days from the date of sending to the Prime Minister or Minister under Clause 97 paragraph two.

Clause 99 Raising a general interpellation under Clause 96 shall be placed as the first member's interpellation on the meeting agenda according to the order of submission to the President of the Council.

Chapter 5 General Debates

Part 1: General Debate for a Vote of No Confidence

[หน้า 29 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 29 of the original]

Clause 100 When a member wishes to propose a motion requesting a general debate, a written document must be submitted to the President of the Council indicating a general debate for a vote of no confidence against the Executive Board, either individually or as a whole, and specifying the matters.

Clause 101 When the President of the Council receives the motion under Clause 100, a review shall be conducted, and if there are defects, the President of the Council shall inform the proposer within seven days from the date of receiving the motion.

Once the President of the Council has verified the correctness of the motion, it shall be placed on the meeting agenda as an urgent matter and the Council's Executive Board shall be informed.

Clause 102 Only the Council's Executive Board has the right to debate and clarify. The clarification under paragraph one may clarify the members' debates one by one in order, or may wait to clarify multiple persons at once.

Clause 103 For the debate, the provisions in Chapter 2, Part 3 Debate, shall apply mutatis mutandis.

Part 2: General Debate without a Resolution

Clause 104 A motion requesting a general debate without a resolution must be made in writing and submitted to the President of the Council, clearly specifying to which person and regarding what matter the factual questions or problem suggestions are directed.

When the President of the Council receives the motion under paragraph one, the provisions in Clause 101 shall apply mutatis mutandis.

[หน้า 30 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 30 of the original]

Clause 105 For the debate, the provisions in Chapter 2, Part 3 Debate, shall apply mutatis mutandis.

Chapter 6 Maintenance of Order and Propriety

Clause 106 Any acts that disrupt the Council meeting are prohibited.

Clause 107 If anyone violates these regulations, the President has the power to warn, prohibit, order the withdrawal of words, prohibit speaking on the matter currently under consultation, demand an apology in the assembly, or order removal from the meeting with or without a specified time frame on that occasion.

In the event that the President orders anyone to leave the meeting, if that person resists, the President has the power to order security personnel or University staff to remove the person from the meeting venue or out of the Council Precinct.

In meetings via electronic media, if that person resists, the President has the power to expel them from the meeting according to methods prescribed by the President.

The President's order under this clause is deemed final.

Clause 108 Calling and identifying the names of persons shall be done as follows:

  1. In calling or identifying one's own name, it is the freedom of that person to use or not use a name prefix.

  2. In calling or identifying the name of another person, the name prefix of that person must not be called or identified, unless that person wishes for the name prefix to be used.

[หน้า 31 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 31 of the original]

Clause 109 Attire for entering the Council meeting or Council Precinct is the freedom of the individual to wear any style, provided they are not naked.

Displaying media or images containing profanity or rude words in meetings via electronic media is permitted, provided it does not specifically attack any individual and does not display obscene images.

Clause 110 Members have the right to attend Council meetings and committee meetings, and no person can obstruct them, except in violation of these regulations under Clause 78.

Chapter 7 Termination of the Council's Executive Board

Clause 111 The Council's Executive Board vacates office upon:

  1. Expiration of the Council's term.

  2. Death.

  3. Resignation.

  4. Termination of membership.

  5. A resolution of the Council to vacate office according to the motion in Clause 30, with an approval vote of no less than two-thirds of the total existing members of the Council.

[หน้า 32 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 32 of the original]

Chapter 8 Council Orders

Clause 112 A Council Order refers to an official directive approved by the Council Executive Committee issued to a member of the Executive Committee or, where applicable, to a member of the Council. Such orders may mandate the performance of duties, the suspension of duties, or the execution of any acts pertaining to the interests of the students, the University, or the Council.

Any order requiring an Executive Committee member to temporarily cease the performance of their duties shall require the additional approval of the Joint Coordination Committee.

The President of the Council shall declare the necessity of such Council Order to the members within one day from the date on which the President signs the official Council Order document.

Clause 113 Council orders must be published to the public via the Council's electronic media within three days from the date the President signs the Council order, unless members request it to remain secret, which requires an approval resolution of no less than one-third of the total existing members.

If members object and request public publication, a resolution of approval from more than one-fourth of the total existing members is required before it can be published.

[หน้า 33 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 33 of the original]

Chapter 9 Final Provisions

Clause 114 If the President requests consultation or a member proposes a motion, seconded by no less than ten members, to temporarily suspend the application of any clause of these regulations in whole or in part for a specific case, and if the assembly approves with votes of no less than two-thirds of the quorum, the application may be suspended.

Clause 115 If the President of the Council rules that any operation is an extremely urgent matter, and the Council's Executive Board and the Joint Council Coordinating Committee resolve approval with no less than two-thirds of the existing members of each committee, by notifying members of the reasons and such operations, it shall be mutatis mutandis deemed a Council resolution.

Clause 116 If there are issues requiring interpretation of these regulations, it shall be the power of the Council to rule, and once the assembly has made a ruling resolution with votes of no less than half of the existing members of the Council, that ruling shall be deemed final.

A request for the assembly to rule under paragraph one may be done by the President requesting consultation or a member proposing a motion seconded by no less than ten members.

Clause 117 A request to amend these regulations must be proposed as a motion seconded by no less than ten members.

A resolution to amend these regulations requires approval with votes of no less than two-thirds of the total existing members of the Council.

Clause 118 An objection to the ruling or discretion of the President can be made in the event that a member proposes a motion to object, seconded by no less than ten members,

[หน้า 34 ของต้นฉบับ][Page 34 of the original]

and receives votes exceeding half of the quorum, except for rulings which are deemed final according to the regulations.

Clause 119 These regulations shall be prepared in Thai and English, with the Thai version being the primary enforceable version. In the event of conflicts, discrepancies, or interpretation issues between the Thai version and the translated English version, the text according to the Thai version regulations shall prevail as the standard.

Transitory Provisions

Clause 120 These regulations shall not affect any documents, notifications, or affairs of the Council that were completed prior to the promulgation of these regulations. For any pending matters, proceedings shall be carried out in accordance with these regulations.

Clause 121 Any documents, notifications, or affairs that conflict with these regulations shall be subject to these rules
